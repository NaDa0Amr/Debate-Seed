Title:Residual Energy-Based Models for Text
===========================================

Authors:[Anton Bakhtin](https://arxiv.org/search/cs?searchtype=author&query=Bakhtin,+A), [Yuntian Deng](https://arxiv.org/search/cs?searchtype=author&query=Deng,+Y), [Sam Gross](https://arxiv.org/search/cs?searchtype=author&query=Gross,+S), [Myle Ott](https://arxiv.org/search/cs?searchtype=author&query=Ott,+M), [Marc'Aurelio Ranzato](https://arxiv.org/search/cs?searchtype=author&query=Ranzato,+M), [Arthur Szlam](https://arxiv.org/search/cs?searchtype=author&query=Szlam,+A)

View a PDF of the paper titled Residual Energy-Based Models for Text, by Anton Bakhtin and Yuntian Deng and Sam Gross and Myle Ott and Marc'Aurelio Ranzato and Arthur Szlam

[View PDF](/pdf/2004.10188)
[HTML (experimental)](https://arxiv.org/html/2004.10188v2)
> Abstract:Current large-scale auto-regressive language models display impressive fluency and can generate convincing text. In this work we start by asking the question: Can the generations of these models be reliably distinguished from real text by statistical discriminators? We find experimentally that the answer is affirmative when we have access to the training data for the model, and guardedly affirmative even if we do not.
>   
> This suggests that the auto-regressive models can be improved by incorporating the (globally normalized) discriminators into the generative process. We give a formalism for this using the Energy-Based Model framework, and show that it indeed improves the results of the generative models, measured both in terms of perplexity and in terms of human evaluation.
